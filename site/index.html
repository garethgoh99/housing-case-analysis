How this is worked out

  • Wealth at exit is the comparison. Every case starts with the same cash and earns the same income. Each month, income minus living costs, utilities and that case's housing costs goes into an invested pot that grows at the return you set. Below zero, the pot is charged shortfall interest instead. When a home is sold, the cash back goes into the pot. Wealth at exit = the pot after everything is sold.
  • Where you live: before move-in, each buying case follows its own schedule of renting or living with family (no cost, no utilities). After selling, it rents at the typical rent until the last case sells. The Rent case follows its own schedule throughout.
  • Utilities apply only in months you live in a rented or owned home. Maintenance (condos), service & conservancy charges (HDB) and property tax are monthly from completion (resale condo), TOP (new launch) or key collection (HDB, BTO).
  • Exit: by default every home is sold in the same month, the latest of each case's earliest sensible sale: end of MOP for HDB and BTO flats, and when seller's stamp duty no longer applies for condos (and not before TOP). A case can be given its own sale date. An HDB or BTO flat is never sold before its MOP ends.
  • Cash back = sale price − loan still owed − agent fee − legal fees − seller's stamp duty (condos: 16% / 12% / 8% / 4% within 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 years) − subsidy clawback (Plus and Prime BTO flats). CPF used and grants received must be refunded to your own CPF account with accrued interest on sale; that money is still yours, so it counts as cash back.
  • BTO value: a BTO flat is bought below market. Its value is tracked from an estimated market value at signing (130% of the price unless you set it) and grows from there.
  • Growth defaults blend the past 5 years of price changes, weighting the latest year most (40 / 25 / 15 / 10 / 10%). HDB and BTO: HDB resale prices. Resale condo: RCR condo resales. New-launch condo: RCR resales and sub-sales. These are rough starting points; set your own.
  • Loans: MAS limits apply. Up to 75% of the price, dropping to 55% if the tenure is over 25 years (HDB flats) or 30 years (private), or the loan runs past age 65. The HDB loan is capped at 25 years. Rates are held flat, so use the SORA picker to test higher rates.
  • Payments: resale HDB pays the downpayment and stamp duty at completion. BTO pays a booking fee, the downpayment and stamp duty at signing (bank loan: 5% more at keys), and draws the loan at keys. Resale condo pays 5% in cash at the option, plus stamp duty at exercise and the rest of the downpayment at completion. New-launch condo pays progressively by construction stage: you pay the first stages until your share is covered, then the bank pays.
  • Stamp duty uses IRAS residential BSD tiers (1% to 6%). Loan checks use an HDB instalment up to 30% of gross income (MSR) and total debt up to 55% (TDSR) at a 4% stress rate. Rough guide only, not financial advice.
